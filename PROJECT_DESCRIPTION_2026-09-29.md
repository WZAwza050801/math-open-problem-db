## English (one page)

**What it is.** The Open Problem Archive is a structured, openly licensed archive of open problems and conjectures in mathematics. Phase one systematically covers five leading journals — *Inventiones Mathematicae*, *Annals of Mathematics*, *Journal of the AMS*, *Acta Mathematica*, and *Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS* — over 2000–2025 (818 papers to date). Each problem is stored as a canonical entry with a stable permanent ID, a rewritten self-contained statement, provenance (paper, year, arXiv link, MSC classification), a status event chain (proposed / cited / claimed solved / verified), and a citation-derived vitality indicator.

**How it is built.** Extraction and deduplication are AI-assisted (semantic retrieval + independent double adjudication), but every published entry passes human expert review. Merges are evidence-based and fully reversible with a complete audit log. Status claims are never asserted directly by the system: they are recorded as sourced events ("claimed solved in paper X"), and the displayed status is derived from the event chain — a design intended to avoid unverified AI claims.

**What is public, and what is not.** The public layer contains only: rewritten statements, bibliographic metadata, MSC codes, paper-time status, and links to arXiv / DOI pages. It does **not** contain verbatim excerpts from journal articles, full texts, or any paywalled material. Internal evidence layers (including short quotations used for verification) remain private. The dataset will be released under CC BY 4.0, limited strictly to our own curated and rewritten content; external datasets are ingested only under compatible licenses with per-record source attribution.

**Why it matters.** Open problems are currently scattered across papers, wikis, and personal lists, with no systematic lifecycle tracking. The archive aims to (i) make the open-problem landscape of top journals searchable and classifiable; (ii) record the life of a problem — proposal, citations, partial progress, resolution — as structured events; (iii) provide stable IDs that future discussion layers (e.g., per-problem threads on existing platforms) can attach to. The curation pipeline itself, and the resulting dataset, are also research outputs on how open problems are posed and evolve.

**Relationship to publishers and societies.** We use only arXiv TeX sources and lawfully accessed materials, link rather than copy, and welcome corrections, opt-outs, and editorial guidance from journals and societies. We see this as complementary infrastructure for the community, and we would be glad to discuss alignment with AMS/EMS data policies.
